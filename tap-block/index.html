Privacy Policy

Tap Block Match: Color Puzzle, a game for Android by onepunchapp. Last updated 15 November 2026.

Tap Block Match: Color Puzzle has no account of its own, shows no ads and sells nothing. This page explains what the game keeps on your phone, what goes to Google, why, and how to delete it.

What stays on your phone

The game saves your progress and your settings, such as the level you are on, your coins, your block looks, your power-ups and the sound, music and vibration settings, in a file inside the game's own folder on your phone. The same file keeps your answer about sharing how you play.

Your save in Google Play Games

When you are signed in to Google Play Games on your phone, the game keeps a copy of that save in your Google account's saved games, so your progress can follow you to another phone. Google keeps it under Google's Privacy Policy. We do not see it.

Sharing how you play

The game shares how you play unless you turn off the Share play data switch in Settings. When it shares, the game sends Google Analytics for Firebase:

  • each level you start: its number and which try it is since you opened the game;
  • each level that ends: whether you won, lost or started it again, how many seconds it took, how many blocks you tapped and how many of those taps were blocked;
  • each time you play on after a level is lost: the level's number and whether you paid with coins or used a Play on you already had;
  • how long you played each time you leave the game.

With it, Google Analytics for Firebase itself receives a random id made for this installation of the game, your phone's model, Android version and language, your IP address, which tells your general location such as your country, when the game was first opened, and how often and how long it is used. It does not receive your phone's advertising ID: the game turns that off. Google handles it under Google's Privacy Policy.

Why we use it

  • Keeping your save, on your phone and in Google Play Games: to give you the game you play.
  • Sharing how you play: our interest in making the levels fairer and the game better, and in knowing which of our campaigns bring players. You can refuse it at any time with the Share play data switch.

What the game asks of your phone

The game uses the internet, for the play data above and the save in Google Play Games. It asks to vibrate the phone, for its haptic feedback, which you can turn off in Settings. It reads the Google Play install referrer, which tells Google Analytics for Firebase where the game was downloaded from.

Keeping it safe

Everything the game sends goes over an encrypted connection. The game uses no servers of ours. We see what you share about how you play on Google's dashboards, as totals and as rows that name no one. We never sell your information. Google may handle it in the United States and other countries, under its own policy.

Google Play

Google Play downloads and updates the game under Google's Privacy Policy. Google shows us counts that name no one, such as how many people installed the game and, from phones whose owners chose to share it with Google, how often it crashed.

Deleting it

  • The save file on your phone is deleted when you uninstall the game, or when you clear its storage in your phone's settings.
  • The save in Google Play Games is deleted from your Google account's Play Games settings, under your data.
  • What the game shared about how you play: turn off Share play data in Settings. That stops the sending and clears Google's id for this installation on your phone. You can also write to us at the address below and we will ask Google to delete it.

Children

The game is not made for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Changes

When this changes, this page is updated first, with a new date at the top.
