Privacy Policy

Knife Hit RPG, a game for Android by onepunchapp. Last updated 24 November 2026.

Knife Hit RPG has no account of its own. This page explains what the game keeps on your phone, what goes to Google, Unity and Meta, and how to delete it. The game never shows an ad by itself: an ad plays only when you choose to watch one for a reward.

What stays on your phone

The game saves your progress and your settings, such as the chapters you have opened, the heroes, looks and upgrades you have unlocked, your coins, what you have bought and the sound volume, in a file inside the game's own folder on your phone.

Your saved game in Google Play Games

When the game starts, it signs you in to Google Play Games, Google's games service on your phone, and keeps a copy of that file in your Google Play Games saved games. That copy is what lets you carry on after an uninstall or on a new phone. Google keeps it with your Google account, under Google's Privacy Policy, and we cannot see it.

The question the game asks Unity at each start

Each time you open the game or come back to it, the game asks Unity's Remote Config service two things: whether this version of the game is still allowed to play, or an update is needed first, and whether any of the game's numbers have changed, such as a price or a reward. The game needs the answer to know whether it may start, so this question goes in every country and you cannot turn it off. It carries:

  • a random number that Unity's part of the game makes for this installation;
  • your phone's model, Android version, processor, memory, graphics (including its graphics chip's maker and model numbers) and screen, its language, and whether it is rooted;
  • the game's name and version, how it was installed and the app store it came from, and whether it is a test copy of the game;
  • while you share how you play (below), the random number for you as a player.

Like any connection, it also shows Unity your phone's internet address. Unity uses it only to answer the question, under Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

Ads, only when you choose

An ad plays only when you tap a button that offers a reward for watching one. The ads come through Unity LevelPlay, Unity's ad service, which picks each ad from Unity Ads, Unity's own ad network, and, until the end of 2026, from ironSource Ads, Unity's other ad network.

While the game is open, the ad service keeps an ad ready in case you want one. For that, and when an ad plays, it receives:

  • your phone's advertising ID;
  • your IP address, which tells your general location, such as your country and city;
  • your phone's model, Android version and language;
  • which ads were shown to you, watched and tapped;
  • what you buy in the game;
  • how the ad service itself runs on your phone, such as its errors and how long an ad took to load.

Unity uses it to choose and show the ad, to count what was watched, to stop fraud and, where you allow it, to choose ads that suit you. Unity handles it under Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

Your choices about ads

In the European Union, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks you, the first time the game loads, whether ads may use your information to suit you. If you say no, you still get ads, chosen without your information. You can change your answer any time in Settings, under Ad choices. To know whether to ask you and to keep your answer, the form connects to Google, under Google's Privacy Policy.

Everywhere else, ads may use your information to suit you unless you turn off the Ads picked for you switch in Settings. With it off, you still get ads, chosen without your information.

Anywhere, you can reset or delete your phone's advertising ID in your phone's settings, under Google, then Ads (on newer phones: Privacy, then Ads). Unity's ads also carry a small privacy button inside the ad.

Purchases

You can buy things inside the game, such as gems and packs. Every purchase goes through Google Play: Google handles the payment under Google's Privacy Policy, and we never receive your card or payment details. The game checks the purchase on your phone and keeps what you bought in its save file, and Google keeps a record of it with your Google account.

Sharing how you play

In the European Union, the UK and Switzerland, the game shares how you play only if you say yes in Google's consent form. Everywhere else, it shares how you play unless you turn off the Share play data switch in Settings. When it shares, the game sends Unity Analytics:

  • each run you start: the hero and the chapter;
  • each fight you win: which fight it was, its boss, how many seconds it took and how much health you had left;
  • each card you pick, with the cards you passed over, and each talent you buy, with its level and its price in coins;
  • how each run ends (won, lost or given up) and how long it lasted;
  • each offer of an ad for a reward (to open the daily or the bonus chest, open a Silver, Gold or Legendary Chest or get free coins in the shop, refill your energy, enter the gear dungeon, the key dungeon or the lair, or get Blueprints on the gear page): when it shows, when you take it and when the reward comes;
  • each ad you watch, with what it earned us (in US dollars, as our ad service reports it) and the ad network that showed it;
  • each purchase: what you bought, the price you paid and its currency, and Google Play's order number;
  • the gems you spend, and on what;
  • each try at the tower, the dungeons and the lair, and each sweep;
  • with each run you start: your talents, your gear, your heroes' mastery, and whether you have the monthly card, the lifetime card or the paid pass;
  • when you leave the game: the screen you were on, how long you were on it, how long since your last tap, how long you played that time, your last result, the day of play, the fight and the run you were on, and how quickly the game loaded and how smoothly it ran;
  • your first steps in the game, such as your first throw, your first chest and the first time you open the talents or the treasury;
  • when you open and close the game;
  • your phone's model, Android version, processor, graphics, memory, screen size, battery level and volume, your language and country, and the game's version;
  • a random number for you as a player, kept in your saved game, so it comes back with your saved game after a reinstall or on a new phone; and a scrambled form of the ID Android gives this game on your phone, which stays the same if you reinstall.

It never sends your name. We use it to balance the game: to see which heroes, cards and talents work, and where runs end. Unity handles it for us under Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

When it shares, the game also tells Google (through Firebase Analytics) and Meta (through Facebook's app events) that it was installed and opened, so our ads on Google, Facebook and Instagram can be counted and shown to people likely to enjoy the game. Google also gets the game events listed above, with the random number for you as a player, and its part of the game notes by itself the purchases you make, the screens you open and how long you play. Meta gets three from the game: when you finish your first run, each ad you choose to watch, with what that ad earned us, and each purchase you make, with which item and what you paid. With them go your phone's advertising ID, its model and Android version, your IP address and the game's version. Google handles it under Google's Privacy Policy, Meta under Meta's Privacy Policy.

If you say no in Google's form, or turn the switch off, the game sends nothing to Unity Analytics, Google or Meta. Only the question at each start (above) still goes to Unity, without the random number for you as a player. You can change your answer any time in Settings: under Ad choices in the European Union, the UK and Switzerland, with the Share play data switch everywhere else.

Checking the time

Each time you open the game or come back to it, it asks Google's website (www.google.com) for the current time, so that moving your phone's clock forward cannot bring gifts and timers early. The question carries nothing about you or your game; like any visit to a website, it shows Google your phone's internet address. The answer, the time, only sets the game's clock. The game also reads the time from Unity's answer to the question at each start. Google handles the question under Google's Privacy Policy.

Keeping it safe

Everything the game sends goes over an encrypted connection. The game uses no servers of ours and no crash reporting of its own (Meta's part reports its own errors to Meta). We see what you share about how you play on Unity's, Google's and Meta's dashboards, each order on Google Play (what was bought, when, its price and the country), and totals of the ads watched. We never see your saved game or your card, and we never sell your information. Google, Unity and Meta may handle it in the United States and other countries, under their own policies.

The Discord link

The start screen has a sign that opens our community's server on Discord. Tapping it opens the invite in your browser or in the Discord app; the game sends nothing with it. What you do on Discord is covered by Discord's Privacy Policy.

Google Play

Google Play downloads and updates the game under Google's Privacy Policy. Google shows us counts that name no one, such as how many people installed the game and, from phones whose owners chose to share it with Google, how often it crashed.

The game may ask you to rate it on Google Play. The rating opens in Google's own window: what you write there goes to Google under its policy, and the game gets nothing back.

Deleting it

  • The save file on your phone is deleted when you uninstall the game, or when you clear its storage in your phone's settings.
  • The copy in Google Play Games: you can delete a game's saved data in Google Play Games, and Google keeps or deletes it as your Google account settings say.
  • What the game shared about how you play: take your yes back under Ad choices in Settings (in the European Union, the UK and Switzerland) or turn off Share play data in Settings (everywhere else). That stops the sending to Unity, Google and Meta, asks Unity to delete what your phone sent, clears what Google's part of the game kept on your phone, and gives you a new random number as a player, so nothing sent later is tied to what was sent before. You can also write to us at the address below and we will ask Unity to delete it.
  • What Google and Meta already received when you shared is kept by them under their own policies above, which tell how to ask them to see or delete it.
  • What the question at each start carried is kept by Unity under its policy above, which tells how to ask Unity to see or delete it.
  • What the ad service received is kept by Unity under its policy above, which tells how to ask Unity to see or delete it; deleting your advertising ID in your phone's settings cuts the link to it.
  • Purchase records are kept by Google under its own policy.

Children

The game is not made for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.
