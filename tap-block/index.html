Privacy Policy

Tap Block Match: Color Puzzle, a game for Android by onepunchapp. Last updated 18 November 2026.

Tap Block Match: Color Puzzle has no account of its own, shows no ads and sells nothing. This page explains what the game keeps on your phone, what goes to Google, Unity and Meta, why, and how to delete it.

What stays on your phone

The game saves your progress and your settings, such as the level you are on, your coins, your block looks, your power-ups and the sound, music and vibration settings, in a file inside the game's own folder on your phone. The same file keeps your answer about sharing how you play.

Your save in Google Play Games

When you are signed in to Google Play Games on your phone, the game keeps a copy of that save in your Google account's saved games, so your progress can follow you to another phone. Google keeps it under Google's Privacy Policy. We do not see it.

Sharing how you play

The game shares how you play unless you turn off the Share play data switch in Settings. When it shares, the game sends Unity Analytics:

  • each level you start: its number and which try it is since you opened the game;
  • each level that ends: whether you won, lost or started it again, how many seconds it took, how many blocks you tapped and how many of those taps were blocked;
  • each time you rewind a lost level to the mistake: the level's number, how many blocks came back and how many coins it cost, if any;
  • each power-up you use or buy: which one, on which level and, when you buy one, its price in coins;
  • each offer to buy a power-up you have run out of: which one, on which level, its price, how many coins you held and whether you bought it;
  • each win that pays coins: the level, how many coins it paid, how many of those were the bonus of every tenth level, and how many coins you hold after it;
  • winning the last level of the game: its number;
  • the first time you meet something new, such as the hand that shows the first tap, a new block or a new power-up: what it was and on which level;
  • how long you played each time you leave the game.

With it, Unity Analytics itself receives when you open and close the game; your phone's model, Android version, processor, graphics, memory, screen size, battery level and volume, your language and country, and the game's version; a random number that stands for this installation of the game, and a scrambled form of the ID Android gives this game on your phone, which stays the same if you reinstall. Unity handles it for us under Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

Unity never receives your name or your phone's advertising ID.

When it shares, the game also tells Meta, through Facebook's app events, that it was installed and opened, so our ads on Facebook and Instagram can be counted and shown to people likely to enjoy the game. Meta gets two game events and no others: the first time you win level 1, the level the hand teaches, and the first time you win level 10. With them go your phone's advertising ID, its model and Android version, your IP address and the game's version. Meta's part of the game also reports its own errors to Meta. Meta handles it under Meta's Privacy Policy.

Why we use it

  • Keeping your save, on your phone and in Google Play Games: to give you the game you play.
  • Sharing how you play: our interest in making the levels fairer and the game better. You can refuse it at any time with the Share play data switch.
  • Telling Meta about the install and the two wins: our interest in knowing which of our ads bring players, and in showing them to people likely to enjoy the game. The same switch refuses it.

What the game asks of your phone

The game uses the internet, for the play data above and the save in Google Play Games. It asks to vibrate the phone, for its haptic feedback, which you can turn off in Settings. It asks for your phone's advertising ID, which only Meta receives and only while Share play data is on; you can reset or delete that ID in your phone's settings.

Keeping it safe

Everything the game sends goes over an encrypted connection. The game uses no servers of ours. We see what you share about how you play on Unity's dashboard, as totals and as rows that name no one. We never sell your information. Unity and Meta may handle it in the United States and other countries, each under its own policy.

Google Play

Google Play downloads and updates the game under Google's Privacy Policy. Google shows us counts that name no one, such as how many people installed the game and, from phones whose owners chose to share it with Google, how often it crashed.

Deleting it

  • The save file on your phone is deleted when you uninstall the game, or when you clear its storage in your phone's settings.
  • The save in Google Play Games is deleted from your Google account's Play Games settings, under your data.
  • What the game shared about how you play: turn off Share play data in Settings. That stops the sending and asks Unity to delete what your phone sent. You can also write to us at the address below and we will ask Unity to delete it.
  • What went to Meta: the same switch stops it. If you use Facebook or Instagram, you can see and clear what apps have sent Meta under Your activity off Meta technologies in their settings.

Children

The game is not made for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13.

Changes

When this changes, this page is updated first, with a new date at the top.
